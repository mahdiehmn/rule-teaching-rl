## Frozen menu and question

The contribution being tested is the usefulness and limits of executable
rule applicability: which states receive advice, which information the
executor reads, and when imitation is withdrawn during learning. State
applicability and the training-wide imitation schedule are different
interventions. Existing action distillation is not claimed as a new method.

## Larger KeyCorridor coefficient and schedule sensitivity

Both S4R3 and S6R3 use plain/count students and five arms on ten paired
seeds. All arms have the same 30M training horizon and learning-rate schedule:

- `none`: unguided PPO with the appropriate exploration background.
- `weight_001`: frozen progress-aware rules; coefficient 0.01 to 0.0001.
- `weight_01`: same rules; coefficient 0.1 to 0.001.
- `weight_1`: same rules; coefficient 1 to 0.01.
- `weight_01_fixed15m`: coefficient 0.1 to 0.001 with the historical 15M
  advice schedule held fixed in absolute rollout time.

Standard arms decay to their floor by fraction .5 and withdraw at .75.
The fixed15m arm uses .25 and .375, respectively. With 1,024 transitions
per rollout, 30M gives 29,296 iterations, exactly twice the old 15M's
14,648. The fixed arm therefore reproduces every old advice coefficient
at a common iteration, while all new arms retain the 30M learning-rate
schedule. This tests the whole advice schedule, not withdrawal alone.
The implementation is `scripts/paper_bulk_sensitivity_20261005.py`.

Primary metric: complete regular teacher-free greedy success AUC at the
common 30M horizon, with the original evaluation convention unchanged.
Secondary: regular 2M/5M/15M/30M milestone evaluations, final sampled
success, early diagnostics, and label/phase/conflict/compute measurements.
The 15M milestone floors to 14,999,552 transitions. It is an intermediate
policy on a 30M learning-rate trajectory, not a recreated historical15M run.
Do not splice curves across cohorts or call this a pure horizon effect.

Frozen primary families: sixteen guided-minus-none comparisons (four
guided arms times two maps times two students), and separately four
fixed15m-minus-standard0.1 schedule comparisons. Use paired training-seed
differences, paired-t 95% intervals and two-sided tests, Holm within each
family. Preserve all arms. A positive difference with adjusted p<.05
supports benefit within that contrast; negative, null and inconclusive
results remain reportable. Constant differences have undefined p values,
consume p=1 only for family bookkeeping, and do not establish superiority.
No inferential family is reported before all its planned pairs are valid.
Selection across the whole exploratory package is not corrected by these
within-family corrections. A winning coefficient is not independent
confirmation; a future selected-recipe claim needs fresh training seeds.

Prediction: benefit depends on map, learner background and advice strength;
no specific coefficient or schedule is prospectively designated the winner.
This finite simultaneous grid informs sensitivity and failure analysis.
Failure to find benefit restricts claims; it does not trigger automatic
extension, new seeds or stronger weights. Completion while unsolved is a
valid scientific outcome. No promise that every method will solve is made.

## Initial learning, resources, and stopping

All new MiniGrid trajectories include separately saved teacher-free
diagnostic evaluations at frame zero and the existing dense early schedule.
These evaluate initial behavior honestly; they do not force a low initial
score or alter the primary regular-grid AUC. Crafter keeps its original
twenty-panel evaluation contract as disclosed above.

Each cell has one initial attempt and no automatic scientific or
infrastructure retry. A failure is retained. Any later infrastructure
recovery needs an explicit logged amendment on the same planned seed,
including its attempt limit and cause. A poor completed run is never
replaced. Ambiguous submission receipts stop admission for manual scheduler
reconciliation; no blind resubmission. No `--requeue` or array `%N` cap.

Core/timing retain their existing resource settings. New view jobs request
30h/2CPUs/20GiB; Crafter requests 12h/1CPU/8GiB; the two 30M sensitivity
arrays request 72h/2CPUs/20GiB. Historical larger-map15M jobs took roughly
12--17h in the existing protocol; proportional 30M estimates of24--34h
are forecasts, not measured runtimes. Live queue/account limits have not
been inspected. Queue time and total wall time are unknown. The 200 long
cells account for about75.4% of nominal transitions and are independently
selectable, so they need not delay the source-study jobs.

Launch uses committed source archives, full source hashes, frozen args,
exact preparation/worker runtime identity and durable dispatch/exit
receipts. Crafter admits version `1.8.3` and the observed Vulcan build
`1.8.3+computecanada`. The full installed version, including the build tag,
is frozen in the manifest and must match exactly in every worker. This
checks within-cohort runtime consistency, not equivalence between builds
or scientific success. Source reaches the cluster by git fetch; no
cluster cleanup. Results stay outside version control.
